## SLIDE 1 — Development of Customers: Why We Need a Recommendation System

**Slide title:** *"Development of Customers — Recommendation System & Subscription Engine"*

---

### Current State: What Shopify Does Today

Lush Protein currently runs the **Shopify default recommendation algorithm**. This means:

- Recommendations are generated by Shopify's built-in engine, based purely on purchase co-occurrence across all store visitors
- There is **no custom app**, no email timing logic, and no post-purchase sequencing
- Every customer — whether it's their first purchase or fifth — sees the same generic "you might also like" widget
- There is **no timing signal**: the system does not know that a Clear Protein buyer should receive a Lean recommendation on Day 14, not at checkout
- There is **no sample or physical cross-sell mechanism** — no sachet dispatch, no pre-reorder engagement

**In plain terms:** Shopify's engine is built for stores selling accessories and fashion (large catalogues, impulse buying). For a nutrition brand with 6–8 core product categories and predictable reorder cycles, it is the wrong tool.

---

### The Three Gaps (What's Lacking)

Use the chart: `outputs/charts/slide1_problem_statement.png`

**Gap 1 — Retention**

> *"Most customers never come back"*

- **77.3% of customers buy exactly once** (4,402 out of 5,694 finals-eligible customers)
- Only 22.7% return for a second order
- This is the founder's stated biggest worry — and the data confirms it
- The default Shopify algorithm has no mechanism to bring them back after the first purchase

**Gap 2 — Category Depth**

> *"They're stuck at just one product category"*

- **59% of customers (3,681) have ever bought only one product category**
- The prize is clearly visible in the data:

| Categories ever bought | Customers | Repeat rate | Avg GP |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3,681 (65%) | 13% | S$64 |
| 2 | 1,411 (25%) | 30% | S$92 |
| 3+ | 602 (11%) | 63% | S$223 |

- Moving from 1 to 3 categories is not a small lift — it's a **5x increase in repeat rate** and a **3.5x increase in GP per customer**
- These customers are not bad customers. They're trial-mode customers. No one introduced them to the brand beyond their first SKU.

**Gap 3 — Subscription**

> *"Subscribers behave completely differently"*

- Subscribers: **62% repeat rate, S$134 avg GP**
- Non-subscribers: **17% repeat rate, S$81 avg GP**
- That is a **3.6x repeat gap** and a **S$53 GP gap per customer**
- Only 713 out of 5,694 customers (12.5%) have ever subscribed
- The system currently has no structured mechanism to nudge proven repeat buyers into subscription

---

### What's Already Working (Don't Start From Zero)

This is important context for founders — the brand has strong signals to build on:

- **D1 customers** (top 10% by profit) generate **57.7% of total GP** — the base already exists
- **VIP customers**: 287 customers, avg GP = S$476 — these are brand loyalists
- **Clear Protein Peach 500g** and **Lean TMT 1kg** are proven gateway SKUs with 27–35% first-purchase repeat rates
- **Collagen-first buyers** repeat at 30% — nearly double the accessories-first rate of 18%
- **65% of D1 Lean buyers also buy Clear** — the cross-sell demand already exists in purchase behaviour. The system just isn't surfacing it.

## SLIDE 2 — The 4-Layer Recommendation System

**Slide title:** *"Why 4 Layers? One Algorithm Fails Every Type of Customer"*

---

### The Core Argument

The question founders will ask: *"Can't we just use one algorithm?"*

The answer is no — and here is exactly why:

**Customer moment matters more than algorithm quality.** Different customers are at fundamentally different stages:
- A first-time buyer has **zero history** — collaborative filtering (ML) literally cannot run
- Someone browsing the product page right now needs a **same-cart bundle suggestion** in real time
- Someone who bought 14 days ago needs a **post-purchase cross-category nudge** while they're forming a habit
- A logged-in 5-order customer needs **personalised recs** based on their actual purchase fingerprint

No single algorithm handles all four moments.

---

### The 4 Layers Explained

Use the chart: `outputs/charts/rec_sys_why_4_layers.png`

**Layer 1 (L1) — Rule-Based Cold Start**
- **When it fires:** First purchase. Customer has zero order history.
- **How it works:** If customer bought Clear Protein → recommend Lean Protein (because 53% of D1 Clear buyers also buy Lean). If bought Lean → recommend Clear. If bought Accessories → recommend Lean (41% transition rate).
- **Source:** Co-purchase matrix built from D1 customers (top 10% by profit) — these are the behaviours worth replicating.
- **Where it fires:** Post-purchase confirmation email, welcome flow email, Day 14 cross-sell email (CS-01/02/03)
- **Why not ML here:** 67% of customers have only 1 order. ML needs repeat purchase history. Using L4 (Item-CF) alone would fail 83% of your customer pool.

**Layer 2 (L2) — Association Rules (Market Basket Analysis)**
- **When it fires:** During an active browsing session, at checkout, on the product detail page (PDP)
- **How it works:** Calculates which SKU pairs appear together in the same order — measures support (how often), confidence (if A then B), and lift (how much more likely than chance). Top rules: Lean Taro + Lean TMT together (confidence 87%), Clear single-serve sachets + Clear 500g (confidence 71%).
- **Where it fires:** "Frequently bought together" widget on PDP, cart upsell before checkout
- **Source:** `sku_association_rules.csv` — 500+ rules from 8,955 order lines

**Layer 3 (L3) — Sequential Timed Journey** *(the main layer — see Slide 3)*
- **When it fires:** After order 1 is fulfilled, and after order 2 is fulfilled
- **How it works:** Based on observed reorder timing per SKU, fires an email and a physical sample at precise calculated days — specifically 10 days before the customer's predicted reorder window
- **Source:** Reorder intervals calculated from 81 Clear Peach repeat buyers and 39 Lean TMT repeat buyers — these are real measured cycles, not assumed
- **Where it fires:** Post-purchase email flows (CS-01 to CS-05) + physical sample dispatch in fulfilment

**Layer 4 (L4) — Item-Item Collaborative Filtering**
- **When it fires:** Only for logged-in customers with 3+ orders (730 customers, 17% of pool)
- **How it works:** Builds a customer × SKU purchase matrix. Uses cosine similarity to find which SKUs are "bought by similar customers." For each logged-in session, returns personalised top-5 recommendations based on purchase fingerprint.
- **Source:** `recommender_04_item_similarity_matrix.csv`
- **Where it fires:** Logged-in account page, personalised email content for repeat buyers
- **Why not use this for everyone:** 83% of customers don't have enough history. Item-CF on sparse data returns noise.

---

### Coverage Logic (Why All 4 Are Needed)

| Layer | Customers it can serve | What breaks without it |
|---|---|---|
| L1 Rule-based | 100% (~5,694) | New buyers get no guidance at all |
| L2 Association rules | 100% (~5,694) | No same-cart upsell, no PDP widget |
| L3 Sequential timed | 32% (~1,390) | No post-purchase cross-sell email, no sample timing |
| L4 Item-CF | 17% (~730) | Repeat buyers get generic recommendations |

**The punchline:** L4 (ML) alone would miss 83% of customers. You need L1 and L3 as the foundation.

### Slide 2 — Bullet Points to Display on Slide

**Context bullets (set up the chart):**
- Shopify's default algorithm treats every customer the same — it has no concept of customer stage
- The right recommendation depends entirely on *where the customer is in their journey*, not just what they've bought
- 67% of customers have only 1 order — any ML or personalisation system fails them entirely on first contact
- The 4-layer system maps one algorithm to one customer moment: no single layer does everything, but together they cover 100% of the customer pool

**What each layer solves (one line per layer — read alongside left panel of chart):**
- **L1 Rule-Based:** Every new buyer gets a relevant cross-category suggestion from Day 1 — no history needed
- **L2 Association Rules:** Customers browsing right now see "Frequently Bought Together" on the product page — captures basket expansion at the highest-intent moment
- **L3 Sequential Timed:** Post-purchase email + physical sample fires at the exact day before the customer's reorder window — converts one-category buyers into two
- **L4 Item-CF:** The 730 most loyal customers (3+ orders) get personalised recommendations based on their unique purchase fingerprint

**Transition to Slide 3 (say before clicking next):**
- L1, L2, and L4 are largely automated once the rules and matrix are built
- L3 is the most operationally unique layer — it requires knowing *when* to fire, *what* sample to send, and *how* to route it through fulfilment
- The timing is not arbitrary — it is calculated from real reorder data from repeat buyers
- That precision is exactly why L3 is the highest-leverage layer in this system — and it is what the next slide is about

## SLIDE 3 — Layer 3 Deep Dive: The Timing Engine

**Slide title:** *"Layer 3: Cross-Sell BEFORE the Reorder Window — Not at Checkout"*

---

### Why Layer 3 Is the Most Important Layer

L3 is the highest-leverage layer because it:
1. Targets the exact moment customers are **forming their habit** (between order 1 and order 2)
2. Uses **physical product samples** — not just emails — to create real category trial
3. Is built on **measured reorder timing** from actual customer data, not assumptions
4. Directly drives the category ladder climb that creates S$10,693 GP/yr in prize value

---

### What Layer 3 Does — Three Decisions

**Decision 1: What to send after a customer's first order?**

Not generic cross-sell. SKU-specific routing based on what they bought:

| First purchase | Email recommendation | Physical sample |
|---|---|---|
| Clear Protein 500g | Lean TMT or Lean Taro | Lean 40g sachet OR Collagen 25g |
| Lean Protein 1kg | Clear Peach or Clear White Grape | Clear 25g sachet OR Collagen 25g |
| Collagen Glow 300g | Clear Peach (protein starter) | Clear 25g OR Lean 40g |
| Accessories (shaker) | Lean Protein (urgent — protein trial) | Clear 25g sachet — NOT another shaker |
| Soy Protein 1kg | Clear Protein (hero gateway) | Clear or Lean 25g/40g single-serve |

The routing is driven by **D1 co-purchase rates** (what your best customers actually buy together):
- Clear buyers → Lean: 53% D1 co-purchase
- Lean buyers → Clear: 65% D1 co-purchase
- Accessories buyers → Lean: 41% transition rate

**Decision 2: When to send the email?**

Day 14 after delivery for most categories. Day 21 for Collagen-first buyers (their reorder window is 42 days — give them more time with the product first). Day 7 for Accessories-first buyers (urgent — they need protein trial before they churn).

**Decision 3: When to send the physical sample?**

This is the most critical decision — and the one that is counter-intuitive.

The sample ships **10 days before the customer's median reorder window** — NOT in the first order box.

| First category | Median reorder | Sample ships (day) | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clear Protein 500g | 54 days | Day 44 | -10 days |
| Lean Protein 1kg | 35 days | Day 25 | -10 days |
| Collagen Glow 300g | 42 days | Day 32 | -10 days |
| Creatine 250g | 66 days | Day 55 | -11 days |
| Soy Protein 1kg | 84 days | Day 74 | -10 days |

**Why not in the first box?**
- Sachet in first order box = received during the excitement of the first product. It gets lost in the unboxing. Customers are not yet thinking about their next order.
- Pre-reorder arrival = customer is now in "what do I reorder?" mode. They've finished or are running low. The sachet arrives, they try it, and it influences the next order decision.
- The sample doesn't just introduce a product — it appears at the customer's decision moment.

---

### How the Reorder Timing Was Measured

This is important to be able to explain — it was not assumed.

**Method (from `EDA/12_finals_deep_dive.py`):**

1. For each SKU, filter to all customers who bought it more than once
2. Sort each customer's order dates for that SKU
3. Compute the day gaps between consecutive orders (`.diff()`)
4. Take the **median** gap per customer (not mean — handles irregular customers)
5. Take the **median across all customers** for that SKU

**Why median, not mean?**
- Clear Peach 500g: median = 54 days, mean = 76 days
- Lean TMT 1kg: median = 35 days, mean = 69 days
- The mean is dragged up by customers who paused, stocked up, or bought twice in one month and then waited
- The median represents the **typical buyer** — the one L3 needs to target

**Sample sizes (real, not simulated):**
- Clear Peach: 81 repeat buyers
- Clear White Grape: 48 repeat buyers
- Collagen Glow: 40 repeat buyers
- Lean TMT: 39 repeat buyers

These are real cohorts with real measured behaviour.

---

### What Happens After the Sample

The L3 engine doesn't stop at the sample:

1. **Order 2 fulfilled + Day 7:** Email recommending a 3rd category (Collagen or a new protein flavour)
2. **Order 2 + 48 days:** Subscribe & Save trigger (SUB-01 flow) — this is timed to fire right before the 3rd reorder, when the customer has proven they're a true repeater

The subscribe trigger at Day 48 is specifically designed to capture proven repeat buyers — not first-timers who haven't demonstrated commitment.

---

### The Category Prize (What L3 Is Trying to Achieve)

| Category progression | Customers currently | Repeat rate | Avg GP | Target move |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 category | 3,681 | 13% | S$64 | Move 5% to 2-cat |
| 2 categories | 1,411 | 30% | S$92 | Move 5% to 3-cat |
| 3+ categories | 602 | 63% | S$223 | Subscription target |

- 5% of 3,681 moving to 2 categories = 184 customers × S$28 GP lift = **S$5,152 GP/yr**
- 5% of 3,681 moving to 3 categories = **S$10,693 GP/yr**
- Each category add = +S$28 avg GP per customer and +17 percentage points repeat rate

## SLIDE 4 — Shopify Implementation & Business Value

**Slide title:** *"How to Build This on Shopify — and What It's Worth"*

---

### Implementation Approach: Layer 3 First

Why start with L3:
- Highest impact layer for the core customer development goal (Silver → Gold-B → Gold-A)
- Requires the least tech complexity — no custom Shopify app, no ML infrastructure
- All the data is already built — the 7-row lookup table (`cross_sell_timing_and_samples.csv`) is ready

**The key insight for founders:** Phase 1 is a **batch inference pipeline on a lookup table**. It is not machine learning. The "algorithm" is a 7-row CSV. The intelligence was built offline from the data — the runtime just looks things up.

---

### The Data Pipeline — How It Works

**Step 0 — Trigger:** Shopify fires an `orders/fulfilled` webhook every time an order ships. This is native Shopify — no extra cost.

**Step 1 — Data Extraction (nightly batch job):**
- Query Shopify Admin API for all fulfilled orders in the last 24 hours
- For each new customer, look up their first-ever product category from order history
- Store: `customer_id → first_product_category`

**Step 2 — Batch Inference (the "recommendation engine"):**
- Input: `first_product_category` (one of 7 values: Clear Protein, Lean Protein, Collagen Glow, Accessories, Soy Protein, Other, Unknown)
- Lookup: `cross_sell_timing_and_samples.csv`
- Output for each customer:
  - `email_day` (when to fire the cross-sell email — Day 14 or 21)
  - `sample_day` (when to dispatch the physical sample — Day 25–74 depending on SKU)
  - `recommended_sku` (what to recommend in the email)
  - `sample_sku` (which sachet to dispatch)

**Output A — Email schedule:**
- Add customer to the appropriate cross-sell flow (CS-01 / CS-02 / CS-03) in the email automation platform
- Flow fires at the scheduled day with the personalised SKU recommendation

**Output B — Fulfilment tag:**
- Add Shopify customer tag: `sample_sku=lean-40g` + `sample_ship_date=2026-07-09`
- Fulfilment team checks the daily "sample dispatch" report and ships the sachet on the correct day
- Alternatively: Shopify Flow app can automate this into a fulfilment workflow

**Output C — Subscribe & Save trigger (Day 48):**
- After order 2 is fulfilled, the pipeline schedules a SUB-01 email trigger for 48 days later
- Email offers Subscribe & Save on the hero SKU the customer just reordered

---

### Tech Stack — Phase 1 (No Custom Dev Required)

| Tool | Purpose | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Shopify webhooks / Admin API | Order data extraction | Native — free |
| `cross_sell_timing_and_samples.csv` | Recommendation lookup | Already built |
| Email automation platform | CS-01–05 and SUB-01 flows | Existing platform |
| Shopify customer tags | Store `first_cat`, `sample_sku`, `email_scheduled` | Native — free |
| Shopify Flow (optional) | Automate tag-to-action logic | Free Shopify app |
| Spreadsheet/script | Daily sample dispatch report | Minimal |

**For MVP (weeks 1–4):** The lookup can even run as a daily spreadsheet-to-email workflow — no engineering needed. A Shopify export → VLOOKUP → email platform import.

### Business Value — Tier Progression

The user-facing tiers are:

| Tier | Customers | Avg GP/customer | Repeat rate | What they look like |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Platinum** | 238 | S$388 | 85% | High spend + frequency. Already engaged. Retain. |
| **Gold-A (Whales)** | 130 | S$300 | 72% | Large baskets, infrequent. Convert to frequent. |

| **Silver** | 858 | S$100 | 45% | Repeat buyers. Upgrade potential. |
| **Untiered** | 4,204 | S$39 | 11% | New / one-and-done. Entry point for L1 + L3. |

**The L3 value proposition — moving customers up the ladder:**

**Untiered → Silver** (the volume play):
- Pool: 4,204 untiered customers
- L3 converts one-and-done buyers into Silver-level repeaters
- 5% conversion = 210 customers × (S$100 - S$39) = **+S$12,810 GP/yr**

**Silver → Gold-B** (the cross-sell play):
- Pool: 858 Silver customers
- L3 cross-sell pushes them from 1-category to 2-category buying
- 5% conversion = 43 customers × S$5 avg GP lift = **+S$215 GP direct** (small at face value)
- BUT: the repeat rate jumps from 45% to 54% — **compounding value over 2–3 years**

**Gold-B → Gold-A** (the subscription play):
- Pool: 264 Gold-B customers
- Subscribe & Save trigger converts Loyal Regulars to Whales
- +S$195 avg GP/customer when they cross this threshold
- 5% conversion = 13 customers × S$195 = **+S$2,535 GP/yr**

**Full category ladder prize (conservative):**

| Mechanism | Prize |
|---|---|
| 5% of 3,681 single-cat reach 2 categories | +S$2,940 GP |
